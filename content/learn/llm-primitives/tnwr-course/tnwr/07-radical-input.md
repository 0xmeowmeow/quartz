# 07. Radical decomposition

A billion people already type this way. **Cangjie** and **Wubi** decompose
Chinese characters into radicals and strokes on a Latin keyboard at 100+
characters a minute — a far larger character set than cuneiform, solved.

- Cangjie: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cangjie_input_method>
- Wubi: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wubi_method>
- Gottstein sign classification — identifies a cuneiform sign by counting wedge
  types in reading order, built so an epigrapher can look up a sign they cannot
  read. That is an input method that has not been given a keyboard.
- Unicode cuneiform block U+12000–U+123FF; the four primitives are vertical,
  horizontal, diagonal, and the Winkelhaken (U+1230B 𒌋).

## Check it yourself

- [ ] Learn ten Cangjie radicals and type five characters. Time the fifth one.
      Write the seconds down — that number is your feasibility estimate for
      cuneiform composition.
- [ ] Decompose ten cuneiform signs into wedge sequences in Gottstein form.
      Write the sequences out.
- [ ] From those ten, compute the minimal pairs: which signs differ by exactly
      one wedge, or by position only? Those are the pairs a low-axis device
      cannot distinguish, and they are what the disambiguation gesture is for.

## The gotcha

> Phonetic input methods (pinyin, zhuyin) are the wrong branch and they are
> what every search result will show you first. The mechanism: phonetic methods
> map sound to a candidate list, which requires you to already know the reading
> — exactly the knowledge a learner does not have, and exactly the knowledge
> that does not exist for many cuneiform signs. The symptom is a design that
> needs a transliteration layer nobody can supply. The lever: structural
> methods only. You are typing shapes, not sounds.
